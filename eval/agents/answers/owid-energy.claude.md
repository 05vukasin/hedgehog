I filtered `owid/owid_energy_data.csv` to individual countries only, leaving out OWID regional aggregates like "World" and "Europe" when finding the solar leader.

**1. Germany: renewables' share of electricity**
- **2015:** 29.4% (188.1 TWh out of 639.2 TWh generated)
- **2024:** 58.6% (290.9 TWh out of 496.0 TWh generated)

The share roughly doubled. That's partly because renewable generation grew by about 55%, and partly because total generation fell by about 22%.

**2. Largest solar generator in 2024**
**China** generated **839.0 TWh**, which is **39.2%** of world solar generation (2,142.7 TWh). The United States was second with 303.8 TWh, followed by India (143.1), Japan (96.7) and Germany (74.1).

**3. World wind generation growth, 2015 to 2024**
It rose from 829.6 TWh to 2,509.5 TWh, an increase of **about 202.5%** (roughly 3 times the 2015 level).

The file also has rows for 2025, but I used 2024 as you asked.
